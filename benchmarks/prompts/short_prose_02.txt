In modern database architectures, optimistic concurrency control offers an alternative trade-off between throughput and locking overhead. Transactions execute without acquiring shared or exclusive resource locks, recording local read and write sets during their lifetime. Prior to committing, a validation phase confirms that no concurrently committed transaction has modified overlapping data entries. When conflict rates remain low, this technique minimizes contention and thread stalls, though frequent aborts under high-contention write workloads require adaptive retry policies.
